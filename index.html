The script does the
filing before you do.

Upload the PDF and Line Notes reads the text layer page by page. It sorts dialogue from character cues, stage directions, and page numbers, then works down each page: a cue in the character column sets the speaker, and the dialogue underneath is assigned to whoever plays that part in your cast list. Recast a role and it re-tags the whole script.

None of it is locked. Drag a zone, resize it, draw one the extractor missed, or select every zone on a page and nudge them together when the scan sits crooked. Anything you fix by hand stays fixed. Budget about twenty minutes for a full-length script, once, for the whole company.

What it reads on a page

Dialogue
The clickable line. Runs of consecutive lines merge into one block.
Character
Short, capitalized, sitting in the page's cue column. Sets the speaker for everything below it.
Direction
Italic, parenthetical, or all caps. Read, labeled, and left alone.
Music
Marked where you need it, so numbers read differently from spoken text.
Folio
Page numbers in any of the usual shapes: 12, xii, Page 12, 2-4-11.

Four facts, one click,
eyes back on the stage.

Set the note type in the sidebar and it stays set, so a run of the same kind of note costs a click each. Click the line, confirm the cast member Line Notes has already picked, add a word of context if you want one. The note carries its type, its actor, its page, and the session it came from. That fourth fact is what lets the report sort itself by actor when the run ends.

The seven note types

Skip
An actor skipped a line.
Para
An actor paraphrased a line.
Called
An actor called for a line.
Add
An actor added words that are not in the script.
Gen
A general note that is none of the above.
Jumped
An actor jumped ahead to later in the script.
Missed
An actor missed a line, or words inside one.

The note

Hands stay on the keys. Arrow keys turn pages, up and down step through lines, Enter opens the note. In the popover, 1–9 pick the cast member and s p l a g pick the type.

One note can belong to several actors at once. It then appears in each of their sections of the report.

Cast members have to exist before you can log anything. A note has to belong to somebody, so Line Notes sends you to Cast & Crew first.

Asked at every load-in.

What machine does it run on?
Whatever is already in the booth. Line Notes runs in a browser, so a Windows laptop, a house Mac mini, a school Chromebook, or a borrowed tablet all work the same way. Nothing to install, no license per machine. The interface is dark because booths are.
How do my ASMs and my director get in?
Every production has a seven-character join code. Send it to whoever needs access and they are in. There is no per-seat license, no invitation queue, and no extra charge per person.
How do the notes actually reach the cast?
The report opens on screen, already split by actor. Print it or save it as a PDF for the callboard, or take the actors one at a time: Line Notes writes each person’s notes into your own mail client, addressed and ready to send, with a copy button for anything longer than a mail link can carry. Line Notes does not send mail on your behalf, and we would rather say so here than let you find out at 23:30.
My script is a photocopy. Will it work?
Only if the PDF has a selectable text layer. Run a scanned script through OCR first, which Adobe Acrobat does in about a minute, and upload that. Failing that, you can draw the line zones yourself in the Script Editor and everything downstream behaves normally.
Why not just use a shared doc?
Because a doc holds sentences and this holds records. Every note here is pinned to a line, an actor, a type, and a session, which is why the report can group itself by cast member. A shared doc puts that sorting back on you, after a five-hour call, in the dark.
What happens if the wifi drops?
Line Notes needs a live connection: the script renders and the notes sync over the network, and there is no offline mode. If your booth signal is unreliable, tether to a phone. The app moves text, not video, so very little bandwidth does the job.
Does it track cues or blocking?
No, and that is on purpose. Line Notes does line notes. Prompt-book platforms will sell you blocking charts and cue sheets with a notes field bolted to the side; this is the other way round.
How do I cancel?
Email hello@linenotes.io and we will cancel it and confirm within one business day. Cancellations are handled by a person here rather than by a button in your account, so it takes an email instead of a click.
